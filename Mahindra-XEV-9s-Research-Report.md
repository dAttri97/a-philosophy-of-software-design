## 3. BATTERY & POWERTRAIN

### Battery Chemistry & Capacity

- **Chemistry:** LFP (Lithium Iron Phosphate) — FinDreams/BYD Blade Battery cells
- **Pack sizes:** 59 kWh, 70 kWh (new for 9S), and 79 kWh
- **Usable capacity:** Not officially disclosed — typically 95–97% for LFP (~57, ~68, ~76 kWh usable estimated)
- **Architecture:** 400V platform
- **LFP advantages:** Higher thermal stability (safer), longer cycle life (~3,000–5,000 cycles at 80% DoD), no cobalt, lower degradation at high SoC
- **LFP trade-off:** Slightly lower energy density than NMC (heavier pack for same capacity)

### Motor Configuration

| Parameter | 59 kWh | 70 kWh | 79 kWh |
|---|---|---|---|
| Motor type | PMSM | PMSM | PMSM |
| Drive | RWD (rear-mounted) | RWD | RWD |
| Peak power | 228 hp (170 kW) | 240 hp (180 kW) | 282 hp (210 kW) |
| Peak torque | 380 Nm | 380 Nm | 380 Nm |

*No AWD option available currently.*

### Thermal Management System

- **Active liquid cooling** for battery pack (shared INGLO platform architecture with XEV 9e and BE 6)
- 18 mm additional protection layer on the cooling plate
- Intelligent thermal management system for optimal temperature across charge cycles and climates
- BMS supports 1.5–2C charging rates (20–80% in 20 minutes)
- BMS tested across 19 Indian states and 50+ charge point operators

### Battery Protection

- **IP67 rated** battery enclosure (submersible up to 1 m for 30 min)
- Aluminum extrusion construction with cold metal transfer welding
- Foam-in-place gaskets for sealing
- Reinforced bottom cover for double impact resistance
- Battery integrated into underbody as structural element

### Robustness Testing (Mahindra-published, INGLO platform)

- **Fire test:** 3-minute exposure at 900°C — no fire observed
- **Vibration:** 30–50g shock load tolerance
- **Water immersion:** 30 minutes underwater — no water damage
- **HV safety:** Certified safe

## 4. BATTERY WARRANTY — FULL DETAILS & VOID CONDITIONS

### Warranty Terms

| Ownership | Battery Warranty | Capacity Guarantee |
|---|---|---|
| **First registered owner (private)** | **Lifetime** | Not publicly disclosed (likely 70% SoH) |
| **Subsequent owners** | **10 years / 2,00,000 km** (whichever earlier) | 70% SoH (industry standard) |

- Vehicle (non-battery) warranty: 3 years / unlimited km (standard Mahindra)
- **Exclusions from lifetime warranty:** Commercial vehicles, taxi fleets, demo cars — automatically limited to 10yr/2L km
- Motor & high-voltage components: covered under battery warranty timeline

### What Mahindra Covers

- Battery cell degradation below guaranteed SoH threshold
- Manufacturing defects in battery pack, BMS, cooling system
- Electrical faults in HV system attributable to design/manufacturing

### Conditions That May VOID the Battery Warranty

Based on Mahindra's Warranty & Service Guide and standard industry practices:

| Condition | Details |
|---|---|
| **Third-party chargers** | BMS tested with 50+ charge point operators. CCS2-compliant third-party chargers (Tata Power, ChargeZone, etc.) should NOT void warranty. Non-standard/uncertified chargers or modified equipment may be excluded. **Get dealer written confirmation.** |
| **DC fast charging frequency** | No explicit limit published. LFP is more tolerant of fast charging than NMC. However, excessive DC fast charging (>80% of total charging events) may accelerate degradation. |
| **Charging to 100% regularly** | LFP tolerates full charge well. Mahindra does not explicitly prohibit 100% SoC. BMS manages cell balancing at full SoC. |
| **Deep discharge (0% SoC)** | Allowing battery to reach critically low levels repeatedly may be considered misuse. BMS has a reserve buffer but deliberate deep discharge is a risk. |
| **Unauthorized tampering** | Any modification to BMS software, battery pack, or HV system by non-authorized personnel voids warranty. |
| **Software modification** | Aftermarket ECU tuning or software flashing voids warranty. |
| **Flood/water damage** | IP67 protects against incidental submersion. Prolonged submersion beyond IP67 spec may void coverage. |
| **Accident/physical damage** | Impact damage from accidents excluded from battery warranty (covered under insurance). |
| **Odometer tampering** | Evidence of odometer manipulation voids all warranties. |
| **Missed scheduled service** | Failure to complete mandatory scheduled services at authorized centers may void warranty. |

### Warranty Claim Process

1. Report issue to authorized Mahindra service center or via Me4U app
2. Vehicle undergoes diagnostic scan (BMS data logs reviewed)
3. Mahindra engineering team reviews remotely if needed
4. If approved: repair/replacement at no cost; turnaround depends on part availability (estimated 3–14 days for battery work)
5. Documentation needed: service history, vehicle registration, warranty booklet

## 10. RISKS & RED FLAGS

### Critical Concerns

1. **Platform-level HV Shutdown Risk:** The INGLO platform (shared with XEV 9e and BE 6) has reports of sudden high-voltage system disconnection at highway speeds. While no XEV 9S-specific incidents are confirmed yet (vehicle is newer), the shared platform means this risk is inherited. Mahindra has not issued a formal recall.

2. **Software Immaturity:** The infotainment UI is not intuitive. Missing physical buttons for AC and volume frustrate users. Haptic buttons misbehave. ADAS calibration has been inconsistent on sibling models. OTA update delivery has been slow.

3. **Bharat NCAP Not Yet Tested:** The XEV 9e scored 5 stars, and the shared platform gives confidence — but the 9S's specific crash performance (especially with 3-row occupants) is unconfirmed.

### Moderate Concerns

4. **Third-Row Comfort:** Functional but cramped for adults on long drives. If you frequently carry 7 adults, manage expectations.

5. **Dealer EV Readiness:** Some service centers are still ramping up. Reports of 2-day waits for software updates. Battery-related investigations may take weeks.

6. **Resale Uncertainty:** India's first mainstream 7-seater EV — no resale data exists. Lifetime warranty (non-transferable at full term) may deter second buyers.

7. **Weight:** At ~2,200–2,300 kg, the 9S is heavy even by EV standards. This impacts tyre wear, braking distances, and suspension component longevity.

### Low Concerns

8. **Interior Hard Plastics:** Below-dashboard areas use scratchy plastic. Acceptable for the price but noticeable vs. premium competitors.

9. **Waiting Period:** 8–9 months for base 59 kWh variant is significant. Higher trims have shorter waits.

### Top 3 Reasons to WAIT or AVOID

1. **INGLO platform reliability is still being proven.** HV system shutdowns on sibling models (XEV 9e) are a safety-critical red flag. While no XEV 9S-specific incidents are confirmed yet, the shared platform inherits this risk. **Waiting 6–12 months for Mahindra to stabilize the platform is prudent.**

2. **Software experience is not yet mature.** Missing physical controls, unintuitive menus, slow OTA updates, and ADAS calibration issues are real pain points. If you expect Korean-level software polish, the 9S is not there yet.

3. **Third row is functional, not comfortable.** If your primary reason for considering the 9S is genuine 7-adult capability for long drives, the third row will disappoint. It works for kids and short trips. For regular 7-adult use, wait for second-gen 7-seater EVs with longer wheelbases.
